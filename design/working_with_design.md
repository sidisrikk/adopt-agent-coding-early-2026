The shift from **20% design craft** to **10% design precision** is the design equivalent of moving from manual coding to Specification-Driven Development. It marks the transition from manually drawing every single screen to defining the strict rules and logic that allow AI to generate those screens for you.

Here is the breakdown of what that actually looks like in practice:

## 20% Design Craft (The Pre-AI Era)

In traditional workflows, "craft" refers to the artisan-like, highly manual labor of creating UI/UX. Because developers needed exact visual blueprints to build from, designers spent a massive chunk of project time acting as human rendering engines.

- **Pixel-Pushing:** Manually drawing every single screen, breakpoint (mobile, tablet, desktop), and interaction state (hover, active, disabled, error).
- **The Handoff Bottleneck:** Producing exhaustive, high-fidelity Figma files and spending hours in meetings explaining the visual intent to frontend engineers.
- **Repetitive Execution:** Designing the 50th iteration of a standard data table or user profile settings page from scratch.

## 10% Design Precision (The AI-Augmented Era)

When AI agents can instantly generate standard UI components from a well-structured prompt, the need to manually draw every screen evaporates. The designer's role shrinks in _total volume_ (down to 10%) but demands a much higher level of _accuracy and architectural thinking_.

- **Design as Specification:** Just as "code is planning" on the backend, design becomes about establishing strict, machine-readable rules. You define the core design system—typography scales, spacing tokens, accessibility constraints, and color variables.
- **High-Leverage Decisions:** Instead of drawing a dashboard, you define the complex UX flows, edge cases, and the behavioral psychology of the interface.
- **Directing the AI:** The "precision" comes from articulating the visual intent so cleanly that an AI coding assistant can accurately scaffold 50 screens from a single set of rules. If the rules are sloppy, the AI generates chaos; if the rules are precise, execution is nearly automatic.

In short, "craft" was about spending hours physically drawing the UI. "Precision" is about spending a fraction of that time building a bulletproof system of rules so the AI can draw it for you.
